# Sources

Technical sources behind the specification, with what each was actually used
for. This mirrors Appendix A of the specification; keep the two synchronized.
Sources were checked on 8 October 2026. No source publishes this entire
specification or a single official CWH woodland blend; the requirements in
Parts 1–6 are original project clauses.

## Ecological basis

- Province of British Columbia. Coastal western hemlock zone.
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/silviculture/stocking-standards/tree-species-select/climatic-zones-bc/cwh-zone
- Province of British Columbia. Western hemlock species compendium.
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/forestry/managing-our-forest-resources/silviculture/stocking-standards/tree-species-compendium-index/western-hemlock

Used for: the wide coastal climatic range (drier inner-coast to wetter
outer-coast) and the western hemlock account of acid organic regeneration
substrates, small balanced nutrient supplies, and continuous moisture. These
support the acidic, moisture-retentive woodland concept while requiring local
site and species confirmation.

## Compaction and physical acceptance

- USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service. Soil Quality Indicators Bulk
  Density. June 2008.
  https://www.nrcs.usda.gov/sites/default/files/2023-01/Soil%20Quality-Indicators-Bulk%20Density.pdf

Used for: the principle that bulk density depends on texture and organic
matter, and that construction traffic on wet soil impairs pore space,
drainage, and rooting. The density caps, porosity, and water-retention targets
are conservative project selections for the specified sandy-loam blends; they
are not quoted NRCS woodland limits. The 900 mm profile and 30 m³ tree
allocation are design defaults requiring confirmation.

## Compost and environmental compliance

- Canadian Council of Ministers of the Environment. Guidelines for Compost
  Quality. 2005, PN 1340, sections 3 and 4.
  https://ccme.ca/en/res/compostgdlns_1340_e.pdf
- British Columbia. Organic Matter Recycling Regulation, B.C. Reg. 18/2002,
  current consolidation.
  https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/18_2002
- Province of British Columbia. Soil relocation.
  https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/environment/air-land-water/site-remediation/soil-relocation

Used for: Category A compost criteria and maturity/stability approaches (CCME)
and the separate Class A criteria (OMRR); the contract requires both. Soil
relocation guidance establishes source and receiving-site obligations; the
environmental QP must determine the applicable criteria and protocols. The
compost provisions do not authorize contaminated fill reuse.

## Nutrient test interpretation

- University of Minnesota Soil Testing Laboratory. Our Methods.
  https://soiltest.cfans.umn.edu/about-us/our-methods

Used only to explain analytical distinctions (separate extraction procedures
for available phosphorus and potassium), not to set BC woodland nutrient
targets.

## Biogeoclimatic analogues

- Province of British Columbia. A Field Guide to Ecosystem Classification
  and Identification for Coastal British Columbia (excluding Haida Gwaii),
  Land Management Handbook 77, 2026. **Master BEC reference for this
  project.** Local untracked copy: `reference/LMH77.pdf`; canonical source:
  http://library.nrs.gov.bc.ca/digipub/LMH77.pdf

- Future Forest Ecosystems Centre. Climate Change Informed Species Selection
  (CCISS) tool documentation, Definitions.
  https://bcgov-ffec.ca/cciss-docs/Definitions.html

- thebeczone.ca. byBEC map, interactive biogeoclimatic unit map.
  https://thebeczone.ca/shiny/bybecmap/

Used for: BEC13 subzone/variant naming and the dry-end reclassification
(CWHdm1/dm2/dm3 replacing CWHxm1/xm2 and the old lower-mainland CWHdm), the
edatopic grid of soil moisture and nutrient regimes, the CWHdm3 site series
used as deployment-context site analogues, and the CCISS future-climate
analogue commentary. Analogues are design aids, not acceptance criteria.

## Municipal contract coordination

- City of Vancouver. Park Development Standards.
  https://vancouver.ca/parks-recreation-culture/park-development-standards.aspx

Used for: municipal design/construction guidance and downloadable
specification sections for coordination. Municipal numerical soil limits have
not been copied into this bespoke specification.
