# Target Plant Palette

User-supplied draft palette of 19 CWH-native forest species targeted for
constructed urban woodland beds, recorded 2026-10-09. Status: working draft —
pending CCISS environmental-suitability verification and professional review
(see `open-items.md`).

Framing from the project team: growing these forest plants in an exposed
urban environment has essentially not been done before at tender scale, which
is the reason a purpose-built growing medium specification exists at all.

## Palette and deployment-context fit

Context fit is a reasoned draft from LMH77 unit descriptions and site-series
positions (`bec-mapping.md`), not a verified rating. UW-M = sheltered mesic,
UW-D = slightly dry, UW-X = very dry; "primary" is the driest context in
which success is plausible with the spec'd profile and irrigation provisions.

## What the palette implies for the growing medium

- The palette spans all three deployment contexts, which the specification's
  differentiated defaults must cover: xeric rock species (kinnikinnick,
  stonecrop, shore pine) through zonal mesic species (salal, vine maple, red
  huckleberry) to moist forest-floor herbs (bunchberry, vanilla leaf, wild
  ginger) that only UW-M can carry.
- No UW-X bed can support the moist-herb cluster (bunchberry, vanilla leaf,
  wild ginger, false Solomon's seal at its dry edge); palette assignment per
  bed must respect context limits, which the spec's Species compatibility row
  now requires.
- Acidophiles dominate (salal, both huckleberries, kinnikinnick, bunchberry):
  the pH 5.0–6.5 window fits, with the lower half preferred for the
  ericaceous species; no liming.
- These are low-nutrient-adapted forest species: the spec's report-only
  nutrient clauses and prohibition on routine fertilization are correct for
  this palette; do not add fertilizer "for establishment".
- Forest-floor biology matters: the moderately organic Layer A, the
  wood-chip mulch, retained clean leaf fall, and retained coarse woody
  habitat (all already in the spec) are the urban analogues of the mor/moder
  humus and nurse-wood substrates these species regenerate on — red
  huckleberry in particular.
- Shore pine plus kinnikinnick plus stonecrop is effectively the 102
  site-series flora; a UW-X bed planted that way is a defensible analogue,
  not an ornamental oddity.

## Cross-reference outcome (2026-10-09)

The palette was cross-referenced against LMH77 site-unit descriptions for the
five dry-end BEC13 units (`palette-bec-crosswalk.md`). Result: a CWHdm3
zonal-forest core (101/103/110) plus a CDFmm-flavoured xeric open-rock cohort
(fawn lily, stonecrop, evergreen huckleberry, nodding onion, columbine) —
matching the UW-M / UW-D / UW-X structure. Consequences already reflected
above: kinnikinnick flagged as a CWHdm2/xs indicator (CWHdm3 102 analogue is
hairy manzanita), and vanilla leaf refined to the richer 110 analogue.

## Verification tasks

- Run the CCISS environmental suitability check for each species against the
  assigned context analogues (CWHdm3 101/103/102/110; CWHdm1/xs for UW-X;
  CDFmm for the xeric herb cohort and as future analogue).
- Berberis repens versus B. nervosa: cross-reference confirms B. repens
  appears nowhere in the dry-end LMH77 prose, while dull Oregon-grape (B.
  nervosa) is named across CWHdm3 101/103/104/110. Confirm which is intended
  for supply and whether B. nervosa should replace or complement B. repens
  for UW-M/UW-D beds.
- Decide whether to add hairy manzanita (Arctostaphylos columbiana) as the
  on-analogue CWHdm3 102 shrub, or retain kinnikinnick as a horticultural
  choice with the analogue mismatch noted.
- Confirm nursery supply of true coastal stock (especially shore pine
  var. contorta, not var. latifolia) for all 19 species.
- Professional review of context-fit assignments before they constrain any
  planting plan.
